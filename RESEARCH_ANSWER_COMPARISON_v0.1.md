# RESEARCH_ANSWER_COMPARISON_v0.1.md

The central question: does ECC-Pass actually produce a better answer, or just a longer citation list? Five questions anchored on now-ECC-Pass objects, five on still-ECC-Fail objects, drawn from Evaluation Suite v0.1.

## Two fully worked examples

**Q030 — "What does CAPM say about the relationship between risk and expected return?"** (anchor: T01, ECC-Pass)

> CAPM states expected return equals the risk-free rate plus beta times the market risk premium, independently derived by Sharpe (1964) and Lintner (1965), and it rests on assumptions including frictionless markets (Modigliani & Miller, 1958). This remains standard portfolio theory content (Bodie, Kane & Marcus, 2018). **However, direct empirical tests find the beta-return relationship is flat once other stock characteristics are controlled for (Fama & French, 1992) — a finding that directly contests CAPM's central prediction and should be disclosed alongside its continued use.**

**Q032 — "How does Modern Portfolio Theory justify diversification?"** (anchor: T03, ECC-Fail)

> Modern Portfolio Theory argues diversification reduces portfolio risk without proportionally reducing expected return, formalized by Markowitz (1952).

That's the complete answer the current evidence supports — one 70-year-old citation, no independent corroboration, and critically, **no disclosure that the Knowledge Base's own Classification data marks a directly relevant assumption (A14, Stable Correlations) as Disputed maturity.** The answer can't surface that caveat because nothing evidence-linked connects it. This is a confident, single-source assertion with no visible limitations — exactly the failure mode Evidence-over-Opinion exists to prevent.

## What this actually shows

Every Pass-anchored answer could state a limitation, an independent corroboration, or a genuine opposing view **using only the cited evidence** — not added as editorial hedging. Every Fail-anchored answer is a bare assertion with nowhere to point for corroboration or caveat, even where (Q003, Q033) some real independent evidence already exists but falls short of ECC's count. **The difference isn't citation volume for its own sake — it's whether the answer can honestly represent uncertainty and disagreement, which is the entire point of Evidence-over-Opinion.** This is the strongest single piece of evidence in this pilot that ECC-Pass status tracks something that actually matters to output quality, not just a paperwork threshold.
